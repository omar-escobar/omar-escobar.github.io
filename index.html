Research lab

3-Axis Drone Camera Gimbal

UTSA Unmanned Systems Lab

What

  • Design and fabricate the shell of a self-stabilizing drone camera gimbal with 3-axis rotation.
  • Create a product designed for efficient manufacturability and assembly.
  • House all necessary circuit boards, modules and wiring.

How

  • SolidWorks for design, 3D printing for rapid prototyping.
  • Brushless DC motors for arm control.
  • Weekly team design meetings to work through changes and improvements.

Results

  • After multiple prototypes, a final prototype implemented in drones showing real-world functionality.
  • Fully self-stabilizing, with full 3 degrees of freedom movement.
  • Carries a 4K camera and laser pointer.
Club project

Monster Mash Rocket

Aeronautics & Rocket Club

What

  • Design and fabricate a rocket reaching an apogee of approximately 1,000 ft.
  • Design a parachute recovery system to recover the rocket with minimal damage.

How

  • Designed in SolidWorks, tested through flight simulation in OpenRocket.
  • Produced plywood rocket parts on a wood laser cutter.
  • Joined parts with two-part epoxy and nails.

Results

  • Launched successfully to 1,054 ft — within 6% of the target apogee.
  • Max velocity 110 m/s; max acceleration 14.5 G.
  • Parachute deployed successfully; recovered with minimal damage.
Research · published

The Influence of Terrain Roughness on Peak Values in Turbulence

University of Florida Boundary Layer Wind Tunnel — National Science Foundation, NHERI Research Experience for Undergraduates

What

  • Assess how randomized terrain roughness influences peak values and turbulence intensity in boundary layer flows.
  • Test whether first- and second-order statistics alone characterize peak wind events.

How

  • Ran the UF tunnel (6 × 3 × 38 m) with its Terraformer roughness grid and three Cobra probes at 45 mph.
  • Compared a homogeneous benchmark against machine-learning-driven sine-wave terrain across 25 trials.
  • Processed the profiles in MATLAB.

Results

  • Turbulence intensity is more sensitive to terrain than peak values — peak profiles matched the benchmark under strict limits while TI did not.
  • Dataset published to DesignSafe under a permanent DOI.

→ doi.org/10.17603/ds2-7xfv-9g89
